What are cookies?

Cookies are small text strings that a website can send to and install on the user's device during browsing, in order to store certain information about the user; this information is then sent back to the web server hosting the site on subsequent visits.

What are cookies used for?

Cookies are used for different purposes: to carry out authentication (for example to access a personal account), to monitor sessions and keep the user's settings when moving from one page to another (for example remembering the language in which the site is displayed), to store browsing preferences, to collect anonymous statistical data (for example to monitor visits to the site), and for marketing.

Cookies can first be divided into two broad categories according to who installs them: first-party cookies, installed by the operator of the website the user is visiting, and third-party cookies, installed by other services (for example Google, Facebook, etc.).

In terms of duration, there are session cookies, which are automatically deleted when the browser is closed, and persistent cookies, which remain until a set expiry date.

Finally, cookies can be divided into: technical or necessary cookies, used for browsing the website in order to provide the service (for example for authentication or to remember the display language); analytics or statistical cookies, used by website operators to collect information on the number of users and how they visit the site, in order to produce statistics on the service and its use; and profiling or marketing cookies, used to associate specific actions or recurring patterns of behaviour in the use of the service's features with identified or identifiable individuals, in order to create profiles grouped into homogeneous clusters and send targeted advertising.

Technical cookies do not require the user's consent to be installed and used, whereas profiling cookies, whether first- or third-party, may only be used with the user's prior consent; analytics or statistical cookies are treated as technical cookies only if they collect anonymous data, otherwise they too require the user's prior consent.

Disabling cookies in your browser

Every user can configure their browser so that it does not automatically accept cookies, delete all installed cookies or block new ones. For browser-specific instructions:

  • Safari: https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/safari/sfri11471/mac
  • Chrome: https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/95647?hl=en
  • Firefox: https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/enhanced-tracking-protection-firefox-desktop
  • Edge: https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/microsoft-edge

Finally, you can enable the "Do Not Track" option available in most modern browsers. Websites designed to respect this option should automatically stop collecting some browsing data when it is enabled; however, not all websites are set up to respect it.
